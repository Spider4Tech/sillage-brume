# BRUME-1024q: a 1024-bit BRUME permutation for the 128-bit quantum profile (prototype, not part of the suite)

Written on 12 November 2026. **Status: prototype, not reviewed. In v0.6 it replaces BRUME-512 in the suite (author's decision, 13 November 2026; `docs/MIGRATION_V06.md`).** Nothing in `sillage/` changes. The decision to build it
belongs to the author: c = 384 for 128 quantum bits, with a wider permutation to recover the speed lost to the capacity, at
32 rounds. The tables and round count are not frozen. The KATs of the 32-round profile are frozen as a regression guard
(`kat_b1024.h`, 12 November 2026); they would change with any change of tables or rounds.

## 1. Design

The design is the BRUME-512 rule (`sillage/select_params.py`) transposed to 256 nibbles. Only the size changes.

| | BRUME-512 | **BRUME-1024** |
|---|---|---|
| State | 128 nibbles | 256 nibbles |
| S-box | AD412650BFEC7893 | the same |
| Group | D₁₂₈ = Z/64 ⋊ Z/2 | D₂₅₆ = Z/128 ⋊ Z/2, same law (a, f)·(b, g) = (a + (−1)^f b, f ⊕ g), vertex v = a + 128·f |
| Generators (reflections s_k) | K = {21, 0, 7, 45, 8, 62} | **K = {0, 105, 71, 76, 72, 31}** |
| Linear layer | support A ⊕ I, 7 terms per row | the same |
| Coefficients | min-conflicts search under constraints (a)–(e) | the same constraints, same search, in C, fixed seed |
| Round constants | SHAKE256("BRUME-v1-RC") | SHAKE256("BRUME-1024-v1-RC"), byte (256·r + j) mod 16 |
| Round | x ← M·S(x ⊕ RC[r]) | the same |
| Rounds | 32 | 32 (§ 4) |

**Selection rule**, fixed before the search; the seeds are in `gen_b1024.c`.
- **Graph.** 400 000 draws with 0 ∈ K, which loses no generality. Constraints:
  - 6-regular;
  - max |N(i) ∩ N(j)| + 2·[i~j] ≤ 5, as in BRUME-512;
  - det(A ⊕ I) ≠ 0 over GF(2);
  - full diffusion in 5 rounds;
  - λ\* ≤ 4.

  Ranking: largest ball at 4 rounds, then smallest λ\*.
- **Coefficients.** Rows and columns with 7 distinct values of non-zero sum. All 2 × 2 minors on the row and column overlaps
  non-zero. det M ≠ 0.

## 3. The 3-round bound (certified)

The method is the one of `../sat/README.md`, compiled with `-DB1024`.
- **Model and encoding checks.**
  - 256 left-multiplication automorphisms of both supports, transitive.
  - Exact branch facts, recomputed.
  - 30 000 real trails (differential and linear, 2 to 4 rounds) with 0 violated clauses.
- **Proof.** Every leaf proof and every completeness proof is checked by **two** checkers: `lrat_verif` (ours) and `lrat-check`
  from the drat-trim repository (M. Heule).

| Bound | Leaves | Result |
|---|---|---|
| C3 ≥ 18 | 361 | 361 / 361, both checkers, 762 s of solver time |
| C3 ≥ 21 | 484 | 480 directly; (4, 9), (5, 10) and (5, 11) through 22 sub-cubes on W₂ each; (6, 10) through 21 sub-cubes plus, for the last one (6, 10, 4), 128 fine cubes on the 7 round-1 neighbours of the active nibble. All checked twice, completeness of every split included |

The hardest cubes are much harder than at 512 bits. Leaf (6, 10) did not finish after 2 h 36 directly (18 GB of proof),
against 32 min at 512. No pattern with at most 30 active S-boxes was found in 15 min, whereas there is one at 512 ([4, 21, 5]).
This hints at a larger C3 at 1024, but nothing is proved beyond 21.

## 4. Modes and measurements

**Modes.** c = 384 and rate r = 640 bits = 80 bytes, which is exactly 5 of the 8 registers of the unpacked state.
- **Initial state.** P(IV), with IV = (rounds, rate, width, 'q', mode) in the capacity.
- **Sponge and XOF.** Domain in the last byte, 10\* padding. The state stays unpacked from one block to the next.
- **Default hash: tree.** Leaves of 8 KiB, 64-byte CVs, a root with the leaf count. Up to 8 KiB it is the sponge with
  DOM_SEUL.
- **AEAD (since 12 November, late evening): full-state keyed duplex** (Daemen, Mennink, Van Assche, ASIACRYPT 2017).
  - **Initial state:** key (64 B, bytes 0..63) ‖ nonce (24 B, bytes 64..87: the last 8 are in the capacity) ‖ profile IV
    (mode 3), then P.
  - **Then a duplex in the SpongeWrap orientation** (since 13 November), with an 80-byte rate:
    - AAD, then text, then length;
    - encryption XORs the plaintext into the rate, which then holds the ciphertext;
    - decryption overwrites the rate with the ciphertext;
    - the tag has 32 bytes.

    Until 13 November the rate received the plaintext, the orientation of the suite. The change follows
    `AEAD_SECURITY.md`; the 4 AEAD KATs with text changed, and the 14 others did not.
  - **Cost:** 3 permutations for a short message since the length block was merged (14 November, option A,
    `AEAD_SECURITY.md` § 6 bis); 4 before, and 5 when the key was absorbed through the rate. The tag is read after the
    permutation of the last text block, which carries the padding 0x01 and `DOM_TEXTE`.
  - **Security: a classical reduction, not reviewed.** An earlier version of this README gave an informal argument:
    - Grover on the 512-bit key;
    - then the bound of c = 384;
    - rate-only absorption as "a special case" of DMV17.

    It is withdrawn. It leaves out state recovery, inner collisions, the numbers of messages and blocks, and forgeries, and
    it does not address the overwrite mode. **`AEAD_SECURITY.md`** maps the mode onto DMV17:
    - the former orientation (plaintext overwriting the rate) was DMV17's flag = true at encryption, so it lost
      log₂(blocks) bits;
    - the SpongeWrap orientation, adopted on 13 November at no cost in speed, removes this loss.

    Since 13–14 November, **the AE step is written** (`AEAD_SECURITY.md` § 4 bis): a reduction of the AE game
    (nonce-respecting, no release of unverified plaintext, multi-user) to DMV17 Theorem 1, **as repaired** (Theorem 1′,
    § 4 quater: two steps of the published proof, F2 and F3, are false as written and are repaired). Bound:
    Adv ≤ (2σ_D + 5)·N/2^384 + 2·q_iv·N/2^512 + q_d/2^256 + negligible terms, under the conditions listed in `SPEC.md`
    § 5. What it is **not**: a proof for the concrete permutation (ideal-permutation model), a quantum proof (Q1 open,
    Q2 not claimed), nor a reviewed result. The repairs (F2, F3) and the partial-flag extension (§ 4 ter) need an
    independent review.
  - **Batched version (`aead_multi`).** It uses the same scheduling as the tree (bitsliced batches of 256 messages, then
    batches of 32, then one message at a time), and is checked equal to the one-message AEAD.
  - **`aead_ouvrir`** is the only decryption to expose: it compares the tags in constant time and wipes the output on
    failure. `aead(…, dech = 1, …)` is internal and does not compare.
- **Scheduling of the tree.** One parallel region with dynamic tasks, the largest first:
  - bitsliced batches of 256 leaves when there are at least 128 full leaves per thread;
  - then batches of 32 leaves when at least 32 per thread remain;
  - otherwise one sponge per leaf.
  - The 128-leaf threshold comes from the cost: a 256-leaf batch costs about half as much per leaf as a 32-leaf batch, so it
    wins above that threshold even if some threads stay idle.
  - **There is no padding.** A bitsliced batch always holds 256 full leaves, and the number of such batches is
    ⌊full leaves / 256⌋. The remaining leaves (fewer than 256) go to 32-leaf batches, or one sponge per leaf. For example,
    with 12 threads and 1 536 to 3 071 full leaves, there are 6 to 11 bitsliced batches, so some threads take no 256-batch
    and work on the 32-leaf batches or stay idle.
  - **When an allocation fails**, the batches fall back to a path without allocation (same outputs). The tree returns −1
    with a wiped output if the root cannot be allocated (check `echecs_d_allocation_injectes_…`).
  - Before the first scheduling fix, hashing 16 KiB took 2.1 ms instead of 0.17 ms.

**Checks (29/29).** Each implementation is checked against the dense reference:

- **What was learned.**
  - The size of the code is not the limit on Zen+. The compact loop fits any op cache and loses 52 %; unrolling changes
    nothing.
  - The limit is the throughput of `pshufb`: 7 per vertex, on two ports, and each 256-bit instruction is split in two on Zen+.
  - Bitslicing removes them. Each GF(16) product by a constant becomes a 4×4 matrix over GF(2), that is XORs, which run on
    four ports.
  - Sharing the XORs common to the four output planes of a vertex (greedy Paar) cuts the diffusion from 55.7 to 34.4 XOR per
    vertex.
  - The S-box circuit, factored by hand from its algebraic normal form, has 22 gates instead of 32. Its two NOTs are moved
    into the round constants, since M is linear.
  - Since 13 November it has **17 gates**, found by SAT (`circuit_sbox.c`, CaDiCaL). The circuit computes S(x ⊕ 3) ⊕ S(3);
    both constants fold into the round constants. Gain: −2.4 % only, because the kernel is limited by instruction throughput
    (about 2.2–2.6 per cycle for 119 KB of code), not by the gate count alone.

    **Why the translated circuit computes the same permutation.** Write the round as x_{r+1} = M·S(x_r ⊕ RC_r), with S
    applied nibble by nibble. The circuit computes C(u) = S(u ⊕ a) ⊕ b, with a = 3 and b = S(3) = 1 in every nibble. Write
    **b̄** for the vector holding b in every nibble, and set mk = M·**b̄**, which is constant because M is GF(16)-linear.

    The bitsliced kernel keeps y_r = S(x_r ⊕ RC_r) ⊕ **b̄**, and feeds the circuit at round r + 1 with
    u = M·y_r ⊕ BS_RCK[r+1], where BS_RCK[r+1] = RC_{r+1} ⊕ a ⊕ mk. Then:
    - M·y_r = M·S(x_r ⊕ RC_r) ⊕ mk = x_{r+1} ⊕ mk, so u ⊕ a = x_{r+1} ⊕ RC_{r+1};
    - so C(u) = S(x_{r+1} ⊕ RC_{r+1}) ⊕ b = y_{r+1}.

    The boundaries:
    - **At round 0**, u = x_0 ⊕ BS_RCK[0] with BS_RCK[0] = RC_0 ⊕ a, so C(u) = y_0.
    - **After the last round** (`bs_lin`), x_R = M·y_{R−1} ⊕ mk, and mk is XORed back.

    By induction, the outputs are those of P. The generator checks the circuit on its 16 inputs before writing anything.
    The kernel is checked against `perm1` on 256 states (check `noyau_bitslice_256_etats_identique_au_noyau_1_etat`), and
    through the frozen KATs.

### 4.1 Comparative benchmark against the classical suite (`bench_comparatif.c`)

Same binary for both sides: the suite through its public API (`sl_hash`, `sl_hash_arbre`, `sl_aead_encrypt`), BRUME-1024q
through `brume1024.c`. Median of 9 series, machines at rest.
- **Remeasured during the night of 12 to 13 November** with the current code:
  - full-state keyed AEAD (3 permutations per short message);
  - cached P(IV);
  - bitsliced tree.
- **Replaces** the first version of this table, which measured the earlier AEAD (5 permutations, 3.89 µs at 64 B).
- Raw data: `resultats/bench_comparatif_1024q.json` (5600G) and `bench_comparatif_1024q_zenplus.json` (2700X).

**Reading:**
- **Where 1024q wins.** It is faster than the classical suite, which is weaker against a quantum adversary, on:
  - every hash from 64 bytes up (×1.2 to ×2), and the sequential sponge;
  - AEAD from 64 bytes up (×1.11 to ×1.32);
  - the tree on both machines (×1.19 to ×2.46).
- **At equal capacity**, its AEAD runs ×1.47 to ×2.6 faster than BRUME-512-256, from 0 bytes.
- **Where it loses.** Only the AEAD of an empty message against BRUME-512 at c = 256 (×0.87 and ×0.91). Its fixed cost is 4
  permutations of 1 024 bits. Merging the length block (`ROADMAP.md` § 1, after the AE step, decision of the author) would bring it
  to 3.
- **What changed since the first version:**
  - the AEAD at 64 B went from ×0.87 to ×1.11 (full-state keyed initialisation);
  - the tree went from ×0.87 to ×1.52 on one core of the 5600G (bitsliced kernel).

## 5. Security of the profile (summary; details in `../WIDTH_ANALYSIS.md`)

**Attack model.**
- **Unkeyed modes** (hash, XOF, tree): the adversary has quantum access to P and P⁻¹.
- **AEAD: model Q1.** Classical queries to the construction, and quantum access to P offline.
- Superposition queries to the keyed construction (model Q2) are **not** claimed.

**Generic attacks on a c = 384 sponge** (moved here from the README of BRUME-512q, abandoned; they do not depend on the
permutation):

| Attack | Quantum cost | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Collision on the capacity, free quantum memory (BHT) | 2^(c/3) = **2^128** | Brassard–Høyer–Tapp 1998 |
| Collision, without quantum memory (classical memory 2^(c/5)) | 2^(2c/5) = 2^153.6 | Chailloux–Naya-Plasencia–Schrottenloher, ASIACRYPT 2017 |
| Collision on the 512-bit digest or a 64-byte CV | 2^170.7 (BHT) | idem |
| Preimage: Grover on the output / meet-in-the-middle through the capacity (a collision search) | 2^256 / ≥ 2^128 | — |
| AEAD key (512 bits), Grover | 2^256 | — |
| AEAD state recovery offline (Q1, Grover on the capacity) | ≈ 2^192 | Heuristic: the classical terms N/2^c and 2σN/2^c under a square root. Not a bound on the AEAD (`AEAD_SECURITY.md`, `ROADMAP.md` § 1) |

**Why c = 384 and not 320.**
- c = 320 gives 2^128 only if quantum memory is counted (2^(2c/5)); with free quantum memory (BHT) it gives 2^106.7.
- The suite states its quantum levels without a gate model, so the worst case is taken.

**What is proved, and what is not.**
- **Random function.** The quantum indifferentiability of the sponge is proved for a random internal function (Czajkowski,
  Majenz, Schaffner, Zur 2019).
- **Invertible permutation.** The bounds of Alagic–Carolan–Majenz–Tokat (2025) are not tight (next point).
- **What the 128-bit level rests on.** It rests on the cost of the best known generic attacks, not on a proof, as for SHA-3.
- **Proved quantum bounds** (Alagic–Carolan–Majenz–Tokat 2025): min(r, c) = 384, instead of 128 for a 512-bit permutation at
  the same capacity. Indifferentiability
  ≈ 2^42.7 instead of 2^14.2. Still far from 128 bits.
- **At 32 rounds**, with the certified C3 ≥ 21: at least 218 active S-boxes, so 2^218 quantum on characteristics, and 2^190
  after 4 free rounds.
- **Structural margin.** The guaranteed zero-sum reach is at least 22 rounds inside-out (Boura–Canteaut), against 20 at 512,
  so the margin is 10 rounds instead of 12. Division property: 11 rounds proved per direction; verified trails for every bit at 12 rounds inverse and 13 forward, so the reach is exactly 11 inverse and 11 or 12 forward, **22 or 23 inside-out, margin 9 or 10** (`dp1024/`, `resultats/division_property_1024q.json`).

## 6. Limitations

- A new permutation, **not reviewed**: its cryptanalysis is only beginning.
  - Done: B, C3, diffusion, degree bound, impossible differentials and zero correlations, 2-round aggregation, cube and
    zero-sum experiments (observed reach 5 rounds), boomerang to 4 rounds (GPU), forward division property, statistical battery (conforming; it is
    not a cryptographic proof).
  - Open: the forward division property at 12 rounds (11 or 12).
  - Missing: truncated and aligned differentials beyond 2 rounds, aggregation of the other 2-round shapes.
- 32 rounds were decided for BRUME-512, whose permutation is the suite's. Here the structural margin is 2 rounds smaller
  (§ 5). Raising the rounds would cost speed linearly.
- Constant time is **measured, not proved** (dudect, `temps1024.c`, 12 November 2026, Ryzen 7 2700X at rest). No leak was
  detected:
  - one-state kernel: |t| = 1.41 over 10⁶ measurements;
  - 32-state kernel: 1.12 over 4·10⁴;
  - AEAD with key and plaintext secret: 0.40; key only: 0.75; plaintext only: 0.52 (each over 5·10⁵).
  - The bench is sensitive: a 300-cycle witness gives t = −357. It is unbiased: A/A gives −0.07.
  - On the Ryzen 5 5600G at rest (13 November), also no leak:
    - one-state kernel |t| = 0.79;
    - 32-state kernel 0.56;
    - AEAD 0.54, key only 0.87, plaintext only 0.44.
    - The bench stays sensitive (witness −1 782) and unbiased (A/A −1.36).
- **ctgrind** (`ct1024.c`, Valgrind 3.26):
  - Covered: one-state and 32-state kernels, AEAD encryption and decryption, tree, XOF and partial leaf batch, all on
    secret inputs.
  - With the secrets marked undefined, memcheck reports **no branch and no memory access that depends on a secret**.
  - The marking reaches all 64 output bytes, so this is not a false negative.
  - The witness (a table read at a secret index) is detected.
  - No memory errors and no leaks.
- Tables are not frozen. Changing the selection rule, the seeds or the constants changes every output, and the frozen KATs
  would then be regenerated deliberately (`brume1024 kat > kat_b1024.h`, refused if the kernel and the reference disagree).
